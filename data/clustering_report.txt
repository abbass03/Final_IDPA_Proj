SECTION 4: KEY FINDINGS AND INTERPRETATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. AHC CHAINING EFFECT (all linkages):
   All three AHC variants produce degenerate partitions: one giant cluster
   containing ~184-188 countries plus k-1 singletons. This is the textbook
   chaining effect, caused by the narrow TED distance range (45-119). Since
   no large inter-cluster gap exists in the data, each AHC merge step absorbs
   one country at a time rather than merging two large groups.
   
   Note: The inflated Silhouette (~0.18) and Dunn (~0.75) for AHC are
   misleading artifacts of the singleton clusters, not evidence of good
   clustering quality.

2. K-MEDOIDS (PAM) - Most Balanced Partitioning:
   K-Medoids forces a complete partition, yielding meaningful cluster sizes
   at k=5: [101, 29, 27, 21, 15]. Medoids (cape_verde, lesotho, germany,
   uruguay, jamaica) are structurally central countries in their respective
   groups. The near-zero silhouette (~0.002) honestly reflects the lack of
   sharp natural boundaries in the data - all inter-country distances are
   within a 74-unit range.
   
   K-Medoids k=6 slightly loses cohesion (silhouette turns negative for k=6)
   suggesting k=5 is the most stable global partition.

3. DBSCAN - Geographically Coherent Micro-Clusters:
   At eps=51-52 (well below the dataset mean of 79), DBSCAN finds tight
   natural groupings with high geographic and political coherence:
   
   - Caribbean island nations (Grenada, Jamaica, Saint Lucia, The Bahamas)
     share nearly identical infobox structure: area, population, GDP, HDI.
   - Eastern Caribbean micro-states (Dominica, St Kitts & Nevis, St Vincent)
     form their own tighter sub-cluster.
   - Central American neighbors (El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras) cluster
     based on structural similarity of their Wikipedia infoboxes.
   - West African pair (Ghana, Sierra Leone): similar economic profile fields.
   - Pacific island states (Papua New Guinea, Tuvalu, Vanuatu): sparse
     infoboxes with similar field sets.
   
   The 89% noise rate at eps=51 confirms that most countries are structurally
   unique enough that no dense neighborhood exists. DBSCAN is therefore best
   interpreted as a method to identify the MOST SIMILAR country pairs/triplets
   rather than a global partitioning tool for this dataset.

4. METRIC RELIABILITY:
   - Silhouette: unreliable for AHC on this dataset (inflated by singletons).
     More informative for K-Medoids and DBSCAN.
   - Dunn Index: highest for DBSCAN (0.91) because the inter-cluster distances
     are large relative to the tiny within-cluster diameters. AHC singletons
     also score well for the same reason.
   - Most honest metric: K-Medoids total_cost (sum of medoid distances).

5. RECOMMENDATION:
   - For a global view of 193 countries: K-Medoids k=5
   - For identifying structurally similar country groups: DBSCAN eps=51-52
   - AHC is academically instructive (shows the chaining problem) but not
     practically useful for this dataset due to lack of cluster structure.
